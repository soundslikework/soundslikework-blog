Over the past few years I've become more and more interested in G&L guitars. Leo Fender's third guitar company, he started it after he left Music Man, which he started after he sold Fender.

Of the 3, G&L seems to have taken the most big swings. Fender obviously became the thousand-pound gorilla of the guitar world; Music Man went upmarket, with smaller production and an ultra-premium price. G&L seemed like it couldn't figure out where it sat in the market, and so you'll find interesting takes on F-style instruments: mixing up pickups and hardware in some unexpected combinations, and at their heart, the tinker's spirit that made Leo Fender such a visionary.

## What It Is

This is a 2014 G&L LE-2 guitar, the second Limited Edition done for the Guitarsbyleo forum. These were super rare - they were only supposed to make 25, and I don't know they actually made the whole allotment.

This body shape is super interesting. In the 80s G&L put out the SC-1, SC-2 and SC-3 guitars, which were basically the old Fender Mustang body shape, but with a 25.5" scale, G&L's MFD pickups and a vibrato. I think they were supposed to be G&L's answer to the Mustang - a student guitar - but updated with Leo's knowledge of what worked for customers, as the original Mustangs are... quirky. Quirky bridge, quirky scale length, quirky switching.

G&L only produced it for a few years and it was long out of production when the factory started looking at bringing it back in the mid 2010's. I think they may have done a run of them in like 2012, not sure. But anyway, they started working out how to bring back that shape and vibe, but maybe iron out a few of the quirks. That mission resulted in the Fallout, which launched in 2015, I think? But while it was in production, they I guess put this run together for the users of the official G&L forum.

They were designed by the forumites at GbL through a series of very rigorous polls over what looks like months, finally arriving at a shared spec that served as a jumping off point for ordering, which had to be done through one dealer, who managed the whole run. I should dig out the posts, people were throwing some really awesome ideas together before they landed on this basic spec.

This one has:

- Ash body Fallout/Mustang/SC-2 shape
- Autumnburst finish
- Single ply black pickguard
- MFD humbucker & single-coil pickups
- DFS vibrato
- Roasted maple neck
- Reverse headstock
- Locking tuners
- Stainless steel frets

I want to reiterate these are all different. There's at least one in blue and another in gold, a few lefties... So each one of these is gonna have its own thing going on. But they all have the MFD H/S setup, I believe most of them have the roasted maple neck, and they've all got the Fallout shape.

I believe the original owner of this one had it shipped to Australia, and then they contacted me when I had an ad up looking for a G&L with a vibrato bridges to complement my hardtail Fallout.

## How's it sound?

I use this thing as basically a replacement for a Jazzmaster. It's a glide guitar. It needs to cover sparkly cleans and nasty drive, piles of reverb and heavy vibrato use. What I was looking for was a punky shoegaze weirdo with a pawn-shoppy vibe.

And for that, it sounds great! Compared to the Fallout (which has traditional pickups) the MFD pickups are brighter, with the neck getting pretty close to a Jazzmaster piano-like sound. The bridge is a little more specialized, but definitely more usable than a Jazzmaster bridge pickup.

<PostImage src={gl4} alt="G&L LE-2" />

The MFD bridge is a little less powerful than a regular humbucker, a little clearer - I won't say brighter, because the Fallout has a JB in the bridge, and that thing is BRIGHT. But the MFD has a less pronounced midrange. It doesn't compress quite the same way when you dig in, either. So the guitar responds pretty differently through an amp, even if the ergonomics of it are basically Fallout/Mustang-esque.

That's of course when it's in series. These have a push-pull tone knob, too, so you can split the bridge pickup and get a more traditional single coil sound.

The thing about the MFD's is that they're a little... MORE than regular pickups. They're a little louder, a little brighter, more bass, than a traditional single coil. In practice this means they reward patience and tweaking, but leaving them wide open all-knobs-on-10 is not usually the way to go.

So I'll usually leave the volume and tone at like 8 and then roll up to 10 if I need that extra push. The humbucker's a little more forgiving about this, but in single coil mode, you really need to be patient with the MFD's. There's lots of great stuff in there, though. These pickups seriously rule.

The neck is gorgeous, too - it's roasted maple, G&L's Standard Thin profile, rock solid and glassy smooth. Apparently G&L only ever did a few runs with the roasted finish, and these ones are dark, chocolately brown.

It's listed as the Standard Thin neck (#1a) but I don't feel much of a difference from my Fallout, which is the Classic #1 neck. 12" radius, 1 5/8" nut width, pretty chunky profile. It might be a tiny bit thinner, but only a bit.

Also want to call out the vibrato. As I said above, I've got this in the 'Jazzmaster' spot in my lineup, and the Dual Fulcrum vibrato is surprisingly sensitive, definitely enough to do the JM thing where you strum and gently move the bar at the same time.

## But why?

This guitar came up right after G&L went out of business. When that happened, I had had my Fallout for a few years and was actually noodling around ordering a custom guitar from them. And when I started thinking about what I'd want - reverse headstock, H/S MFD pickups, vibrato - it started to look a lot like these LE-2's. So when this one came up, and I knew I'd never get my custom, it felt like fate, or close enough to it.

The one thing I can say about this is that it does feel like a shop run. I think this was the only time the G&L factory dealt with roasted maple like this, and it kind of shows. TThe finish on the neck is pretty thick and for some reason, applied to the fretboard, which I've never seen on a neck like this before. Everything else feels really polished - the body feels the same as my Fallout, the vibrato works perfectly, everything is _built_ correctly. It's just that kind of weird vibe to the neck.

Its stupid comfortable, though - the compact body sits really well, upper fret access is great, and the full scale length means it responds with all the snap and body you'd expect.

I DO wish it had one of the crinkle black aluminium pickguards G&L used to do. Maybe I'll be able to commission an anodized one from Herad for it.
