When I was still in high school, I started collecting guitars, and came across one in the Super Shopper. At the time I had an SG and a Strat, but I was dying for a tough Ibanez superstrat.

What showed up for $500 if I was willing to drive to Barrie was a 1989 USA Custom Jackson Soloist that had been through hell and back. It was beat to shit, but I loved that guitar. It had been built for Terry Baker, the lead singer of 80s rock band Bonham, who was from Canada. Somehow the guitar had made its way to Barrie, to a trailer in back of a rental building.

Anyway, that guitar was my first introduction to what's become my favorite pickup setup on an electric: the humbucker - single setup.

No, YOU only have a crappy picture of the only guitar you miss.

That Jackson had a J90C (and later a J50BC) in the bridge, but a very Hendrixy, clear single coil in the neck, and I found between the two I could just get... kind of anything I needed. Rhythm on the bridge, leads on the neck; easy peasy. I always have found neck humbuckers a little flubby, so having a single coil in that position always made extra sense to tighten up the bass and cut a little better.

You see HSS setups a lot more commonly, but I find the middle pickup gets in the way of my picking hand, and the sound of the neck + bridge together is really special, moreso than either neck + mid or bridge + mid.

Eventually, I forgot how special that Jackson was and sold it (if you know where Jackson Soloist J5475 is, please drop me a line) but I've always loved that pickup setup.

At the moment I've got two G&Ls with this setup - a Fallout and LE-2. These raise the stakes even more - besides the clear neck, thick bridge and blendy middle, they both have coil-cuts on the bridge. The Fallout in particular sounds like a really great single-coil with the cut on the bridge.

<Image src={gls} alt="Two G&L guitars" />

I was always confused by the opposite of this setup, like you'd see in a Tele Custom... until I spent some time with a Tele, which had a neck pickup I did NOT get along with. Now I get it - the bridge pickup of a Tele is a total vibe, but having a humbucker (or a wide range humbucker) gives a lot more, I would expect, useful variation.

I've never played a Tele Custom, but I'd really like to. Having an H-S guitar and an S-H guitar right next to each other kind of feels like the best of both worlds now, ha.
